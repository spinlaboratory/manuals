High-field EPR spectrometers operate at microwave frequencies above 100 GHz, typically at D-Band (~130 GHz) or higher frequencies, in conjunction with superconducting magnets generating fields of 5 T or more. At these field strengths, the electron Zeeman interaction dominates over hyperfine interactions, providing excellent g-factor resolution for paramagnetic species with closely spaced g-values.

High-field EPR is particularly valuable for:
- Resolving g-anisotropy in transition metal complexes and organic radicals
- Orientation-selective pulsed dipolar spectroscopy (DEER/PELDOR)
- Studying zero-field splitting in high-spin systems
- Dynamic nuclear polarization (DNP) experiments at high fields

The modular X-Band IF platform can serve as the frequency reference and control system for high-field EPR spectrometers through the appropriate high-field frequency extension module.
