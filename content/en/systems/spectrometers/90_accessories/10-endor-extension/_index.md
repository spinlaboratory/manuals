Electron-Nuclear Double Resonance (ENDOR) spectroscopy is a technique that combines EPR with nuclear magnetic resonance (NMR) to obtain high-resolution hyperfine coupling information. By irradiating the sample simultaneously with microwave (EPR) and radiofrequency (RF) pulses, ENDOR allows the detection of nuclear transitions that are otherwise too weak to resolve in a conventional EPR spectrum.

## Principle of Operation

In an ENDOR experiment, the EPR transition is partially saturated by a microwave pulse sequence while an RF pulse drives nuclear spin transitions. When the RF frequency matches a nuclear transition frequency, a change in the EPR signal is observed. This enables precise measurement of hyperfine and nuclear quadrupole couplings.

ENDOR is particularly useful for:
- Characterizing the local nuclear environment of paramagnetic metal centers in enzymes and catalysts
- Determining the structure of organic radicals
- Measuring weak hyperfine couplings that are unresolved in the EPR spectrum

## ENDOR Extension for the X-Band IF System

The ENDOR extension adds a radiofrequency channel to the EPR spectrometer. The RF pulses are generated by a waveform generator and amplified by a broadband RF power amplifier before being delivered to the ENDOR coil integrated into or attached to the EPR probe.
